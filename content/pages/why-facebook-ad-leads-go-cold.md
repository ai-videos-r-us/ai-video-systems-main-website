Facebook ad leads go cold because a form fill is a moment of curiosity, and moments expire. Across 5.7 million leads, InsideSales found conversion was 8 times higher when the first call came within five minutes, yet under 1% of first attempts happen that fast. The leads were usually fine when they arrived. They went cold in the queue.

The fix is speed and routing, not a different ad.

## Why do leads go cold so quickly?

Three things change in the minutes after someone submits a form:

1. **Availability.** They are at their phone now. An hour later they are in a meeting; a day later they aren't.
2. **Context.** They remember what they asked and why. A call at five minutes continues a conversation; a call on Thursday starts a cold one.
3. **Comparison.** High-consideration buyers enquire with more than one business. The first to respond sets the frame everyone else is compared against.

The 2021 InsideSales data is stark. Under 15% of first attempts happen within the first day, 57.1% wait longer than a week, and 77% of leads are never responded to at all. This isn't new: a 2011 Harvard Business Review audit by Oldroyd, McElheran and Elkington concluded that most companies were not responding to web leads nearly fast enough. Our [speed-to-lead article](/blog/speed-to-lead-5-minute-rule) has the full breakdown.

## Why is it worse for Facebook leads?

Facebook and Instagram lead forms are quick to fill: often two taps with details prefilled. That makes them cheap to generate and low in commitment, so the person is less invested and more likely to forget the enquiry. It also makes the number on the form more likely to be a number they don't answer from unknown callers. It's why a good [cost per lead](/blog/cpl-vs-cac) can sit beside weak sales: the cost is per form, and the value depends on what happens next.

## How do you stop leads going cold?

Put a speed-to-lead system in place with four parts:

1. **Instant notification to a person**, not an email inbox. A text or app alert to whoever calls, the moment the lead lands.
2. **An automatic first text within a minute**, by name, with a booking link. It's the answer if they won't pick up.
3. **A response standard with an owner.** For example: first call within five minutes in business hours. Someone is accountable for it, and it appears on the marketing scorecard.
4. **Persistence.** The same InsideSales study found seven or more follow-up attempts produced 15% more connections, and 81% of sellers gave up at five or fewer.

Measure median time to first contact weekly. If you don't know it, that's the finding.

## When is it not a follow-up problem?

If leads were contacted quickly and still went nowhere, look at the other links in the chain: booking, show rate, qualification, close.
