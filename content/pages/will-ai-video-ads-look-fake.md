AI video ads look fake and damage a brand when they're used to fake proof or published without review. They don't when they're built from your real proof and expertise, follow brand rules, and are approved by a human before they go live. The line isn't AI versus real. It's honest versus deceptive.

Your buyers won't punish you for how a video was made. They'll punish you for being misled by it.

## What makes AI video look fake?

Four things, all avoidable:

1. **Generic output.** Stock-looking presenters and vague scripts that could be about any business. They feel fake because they're about nobody.
2. **Fake proof.** A generated "customer" giving a testimonial, or invented results. This is the fastest way to lose trust.
3. **Off-brand details.** Wrong uniforms, wrong tools, wrong workmanship. A kitchen fitter's audience spots a badly fitted worktop instantly.
4. **No review.** Nobody checked facts, claims or tone before it published.

## What are the five guardrails?

1. **Start from real material.** Scripts come from your actual proof, your customers' real questions and the objections your sales team hears.
2. **Never generate proof.** Real customer footage, real reviews and real results stay real. AI supplies presenters, voice, explainer visuals and edits, not testimonials.
3. **Brand rules.** Tone, wording, visual standards and what you never say are written down and applied to every piece.
4. **Factual guardrails.** Every number, price, claim and result is checked against a source before it publishes, and regulated claims are checked against your regulator's rules.
5. **Human approval.** Nothing goes live unapproved.

## What should you never generate?

- A fake customer, employee or expert presented as real
- Before-and-after images of work you didn't do
- Invented results, awards, reviews or credentials
- A generated voice or face of a real person without their permission
- Anything a reasonable viewer would take as real footage of a real event, with no label

## Does the category change the risk?

Yes. In sensitive or regulated categories (funeral services, healthcare, legal, financial advice) the tone and the claims carry more weight. The best-performing message in our funeral-plan account was about the real reason people buy, not wanting to leave a burden on family, and it was a messaging change, not a format change. Regulated categories also carry advertising rules on claims and outcomes, so check yours.

## How do you tell if it's working, not hurting?

Look past views. Watch comments and sentiment for "is this real?" reactions, then check what matters commercially: enquiry quality, show rates and cost per customer. [Cost per lead can hide a brand problem](/blog/cpl-vs-cac), so track to customers. For Ironclad Finance, an AI-led campaign produced 5M+ views and 74 enquiries in seven weeks, on a business with real proof behind it.
