Lead generation for a UK service business follows the same logic as anywhere: earn trust before the enquiry, call within minutes, track to signed jobs. What changes is the rulebook and the marketplace: UK consent rules for texts and emails, UK advertising standards, platforms such as Checkatrade, and economics measured in pounds. This page covers what differs, for firms spending £5,000 or more a month on ads.

Only what genuinely differs is here; everything else is in our [guides](/guides).

## What's different about following up leads in the UK?

Speed to lead matters as much here as anywhere: across 5.7 million leads, conversion was 8 times higher when the first call came within five minutes ([the data](/blog/speed-to-lead-5-minute-rule)). But automated follow-up by text and email is governed by the Privacy and Electronic Communications Regulations (PECR).

According to the Information Commissioner's Office, you normally need consent before sending unsolicited marketing emails or texts to individuals, and the consent must be freely given, specific and informed. There's a limited "soft opt-in" for a business's own recent customers, and sole traders and some partnerships are treated as individuals rather than companies ([ICO guidance](https://ico.org.uk/for-organisations/direct-marketing-and-privacy-and-electronic-communications/guide-to-pecr/electronic-and-telephone-marketing/electronic-mail-marketing/)). So your lead forms should capture clear consent to be contacted, and every automated message should offer an opt-out. Check the ICO guidance for your exact case.

## What are the UK marketplace alternatives?

UK firms compare their own ads with platforms such as Checkatrade, MyBuilder and Bark. The method is the same as in the US: total cost from each source divided by signed jobs from that source, over several months. Check each platform's current membership and lead terms, because they change and vary by trade. Our [alternatives guide](/alternatives/angi-homeadvisor-checkatrade) has the full comparison method, and it applies to a kitchen fitter as much as a remodeller.

## What about advertising rules?

UK advertising is subject to the CAP Code, enforced by the Advertising Standards Authority, and some sectors (funeral plans, financial promotions, healthcare, legal services) carry additional regulator rules. Every piece we make is approved by you before it publishes; confirm the rules for your sector.
