If you spend $5,000 a month on Facebook ads and the leads never pick up, fix these in order: how fast you call, what the form asks, and what the ad promises. Speed comes first because it's the cheapest fix and the biggest lever: conversion was 8 times higher when the first call came within five minutes in a study of 5.7 million leads. Change targeting and budget last.

Most owners change the ads first because that's what they can see. It's usually the wrong first move.

## What should you fix first? The order

**1. Speed and routing (this week).** Measure your median time from form to first call. Then set up instant alerts to a real person and an automatic text within a minute. Details are in [why leads go cold](/guides/why-facebook-ad-leads-go-cold).

**2. Persistence and channel mix (this week).** Attempt more than once, at different times of day, by call, text and email. Seven or more attempts produced 15% more connections in the same study, and 81% of sellers gave up at five or fewer.

**3. The lead form (next two weeks).** Add one or two qualifying questions (timeline, budget range, location). It cuts volume and raises the share of real, reachable people. Test a booking step instead of a form.

**4. The promise in the ad.** If the ad offers something free or instant, people who want that, not a conversation with you, respond. Align the ad with the actual next step.

**5. Targeting and budget (last).** These matter, but only after the four above. [Spending more first just multiplies the leak](/blog/spending-more-on-ads-wrong-fix).

## How do you tell which step is your problem?

Take the last 50 leads and record: contacted or not, minutes to first attempt, number of attempts, answered or not, wrong or fake number. A tally usually shows the answer in ten minutes:

| What you see | Likely cause | Fix |
|---|---|---|
| Median first attempt over an hour | Speed | Step 1 |
| Gave up after 1 to 3 attempts | Persistence | Step 2 |
| Many wrong or dead numbers | Form friction | Step 3 |
| Reached, but "I only wanted the freebie" | Ad promise | Step 4 |
| Reached, qualified, no sale | Not a lead-response problem | [Walk the revenue chain](/blog/leads-but-no-sales) |

## Why does cost per lead hide all of this?

Because cost per lead stops at the form. It looks healthy whether the person answers or not. [Cost per customer is the metric that reveals the leak](/blog/cpl-vs-cac), and it needs lead-to-sale tracking. Without that, the platform dashboard shows a working account while your phone tells a different story.
